### 1. The Native Mobile Export (Manual & Capped)
Inside the mobile app, WhatsApp lets you email individual chats as a raw text file (`.txt`). While free, it does not support bulk exporting, caps messages at 10,000 to 40,000 lines, excludes large media bundles, and requires hours of manual formatting if you want to inspect records in Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets.

### 2. Bulky Desktop Software (Expensive & Invasive)
Desktop programs like iMazing, ChatMover, or Coolmuster require tethering your phone to a PC with a USB cable, reading raw local databases, or requesting complete device backups. They are often bloated, charge steep recurring monthly subscriptions ($30–$60/year), and demand invasive operating system permissions.

### 3. Dedicated Browser Extensions (Fast, Local & Seamless)
Browser extensions hook directly into an active, authenticated **WhatsApp Web** session. They run locally in Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, or Brave, reading only what is already rendered in your browser and compiling it into formatted spreadsheets (XLSX, CSV) or interactive offline HTML transcripts.
