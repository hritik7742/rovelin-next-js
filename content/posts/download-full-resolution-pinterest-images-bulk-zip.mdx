## Why Most Pinterest Downloaders Deliver Blurry Results

To understand why so many Pinterest downloads end up low-quality, consider how the platform's Content Delivery Network (CDN) operates:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               Pinterest Image CDN Pipeline             │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Original Upload (e.g. 4000 x 3000px HD)                │
│   ├──> i.pinimg.com/originals/ ... (Full HD Source)    │
│   ├──> i.pinimg.com/736x/      ... (Expanded View)     │
│   ├──> i.pinimg.com/474x/      ... (Medium Thumbnail)  │
│   └──> i.pinimg.com/236x/      ... (Feed Thumbnail)    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

When you view a board with 80 pins, Pinterest loads the small `236x` and `474x` thumbnail variants to conserve bandwidth.

If an extension or downloader simply grabs the `<img>` `src` attribute rendered in the DOM, it captures that compressed thumbnail. The result? When you zoom in on a moodboard or print a poster, the image looks fuzzy and pixelated.
