### <h2>Understanding the Need for Format Conversion</h2>

Before diving into the conversion process, it’s important to understand why this task even exists. Here’s a quick breakdown of the two formats:

#### <h3>What is WebP?</h3>
Developed by Google, WebP supports both lossy and lossless compression. It’s designed to reduce file sizes while maintaining high image quality. WebP also supports transparency (like PNG) and animation (like GIF), making it a versatile choice for modern web use.

#### <h3>What is JPG?</h3>
JPG (or JPEG) is a widely adopted format for photographs and complex images. It uses lossy compression to reduce file size, but it doesn’t support transparency. Its strength lies in broad compatibility—most printers, social media platforms, and older software systems prefer JPG over newer formats like WebP.

#### <h3>When to Convert WebP to JPG</h3>
You might need to convert WebP to JPG in these scenarios:
- **Legacy software**: Older graphic design tools or photo editors might not support WebP.
- **Email or document attachments**: Some email clients struggle with WebP files.
- **Print media**: Printers and publishing platforms often require JPG, TIFF, or PNG.
- **Social media uploads**: While most platforms now accept WebP, exceptions exist—especially for stories or ads.
- **Client or customer compatibility**: If working with stakeholders who lack WebP support, JPG ensures universal accessibility.

Use tools like **Lightroom** or **Photoshop** to compress WebP files *before* conversion. Smaller files convert faster and reduce strain on your browser.
