## Why Oversized WebP Images Are a Problem

WebP files are already touted for their superior compression ratios compared to JPEG and PNG. But even optimized WebP images can become problematic when used incorrectly. Here’s what happens when you ignore file size management:

### 1. **Crushing Page Load Speeds**
Every additional kilobyte of image data adds seconds to load times. On mobile networks, a 2MB WebP image might take 15–25 seconds to download. This directly impacts bounce rates—Google reports that a 3-second load time threshold exists beyond which 53% of mobile users abandon sites.

**Example:** An e-commerce product page with 10 unoptimized WebP images (totaling 8MB) could take over a minute to load on a 3G connection. That’s a lost sales opportunity.

### 2. **Inflated Hosting Bills**
Cloud storage and bandwidth costs scale with file sizes. A photo-heavy portfolio site using 200 unoptimized WebP images might pay $200/month in hosting fees. The same site with compressed images could drop that expense by 50–70%.

### 3. **Quality-Size Trade-Off Dilemmas**
Most compression tools force a painful choice: either high-quality large files or low-quality small ones. This trade-off breaks when you need images to look crisp but load fast—like in responsive design layouts where multiple breakpoints are required.
