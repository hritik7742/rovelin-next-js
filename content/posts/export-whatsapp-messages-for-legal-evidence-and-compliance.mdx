## Why Screenshots are Inadequate for Legal and Compliance Standards

When disputes arise, the intuitive reaction is to snap dozens of screenshots on a smartphone. While screenshots might provide initial reference, judges, opposing counsel, and auditors routinely challenge them for several reasons:

### 1. Absence of Verification Metadata
A phone screenshot only shows the contact name stored in your address book (which anyone can modify in 5 seconds). It rarely displays the full international dialing code, sender identifier, or cryptographic message delivery timestamps.

### 2. Disjointed and Fragmented Context
Screenshots capture isolated snippets. Opposing parties can argue that critical messages immediately preceding or following the screenshot were deliberately omitted to distort the context of the conversation.

### 3. Ease of Fabrication
With dozens of free fake-message generator web apps available online, screenshots carry little evidentiary weight unless backed by structured, contiguous raw logs or digital transcripts.

### 4. Loss of Associated Media
Voice notes, contracts sent as PDFs, photographic evidence of damage, and video recordings cannot be preserved in a screenshot. Attempting to play an audio note from a static picture is impossible.

## What Makes an Admissible Digital Chat Archive?

To withstand legal scrutiny under Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE 901/902 in the United States) or standard civil litigation guidelines across the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia, electronic evidence should ideally satisfy four criteria:

1. **Chronological Completeness**: An uninterrupted thread from a defined starting point to an ending point without missing middle segments.
2. **Detailed Timestamps & Identifiers**: Exact dates, hours, minutes, and seconds along with full phone numbers (e.g., `+1 (555) 234-5678`), not just nickname aliases.
3. **Preservation of Chain of Custody**: Evidence must be extracted in a manner that proves third parties did not manipulate or alter the data during transit.
4. **Verifiable Media Attachments**: Photos, documents, and voice recordings must remain linked to the exact timestamp and message text where they were shared.
